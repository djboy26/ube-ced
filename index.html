    How it’s counted

    CED = e (I − A)−1 f

    CED is cumulative energy demand. Here e is the energy each industry takes in, A is every industry’s recipe and f is what the basket asks for. Steel needs electricity and electricity needs steel, so the chain never ends, and Leontief’s inverse adds up the whole loop in one step. Healthcare, still provisional, is counted that way, split by the industry that uses the energy. The other nine roots add up their steps.

    Notes on the numbers

    • Every figure is primary energy for one person for one year, in kWh: the energy taken from nature along the whole chain. It comes from model v5, built on Statistics Denmark’s 2023 tables.
    • Electricity from fossil fuel counts 2.5 kWh of primary energy for each kWh delivered. Denmark’s 2023 grid counts 1.6, and sun and wind count 1.0.
    • Healthcare is provisional, and so are roads and rails. Healthcare’s four branches split the model’s own input–output result by the industry that uses the energy.
    • Denmark is the model’s mean of Copenhagen, Odense and Aalborg. Town climate and sun come from PVGIS 5.2, 2005 to 2020. Its coarse grid reads coastal towns a little mild, so their heating may be understated.
    • A number you enter moves its kWh in proportion, which is how the model itself treats that quantity. Food is grams eaten, and the model adds what is lost between farm and plate. Shops and cold chain follow the food that is counted. Vehicle building follows the km. Heating, extra warmth and ventilation follow the m² of the home. Roads, upkeep, hygiene and healthcare are single sums in the model and take no number.
    • Away from the starting basket, the 90% range is the published range scaled with the total.
    • Widths and kWh on the roots are the model’s. The step names and tip names are a reading of each supply chain. The fans under district heating and grid power name the largest 2023 sources in the Danish Energy Agency’s statistics.
    • A wind share is counted at 900 kWh a year, Middelgrunden’s average from 2016 to 2024. A 400 watt panel stands at its best tilt with 14% system loss. Both set electricity made beside primary energy used, kWh for kWh.
    • All figures await independent checking. The code and the data are open.
